## LinkedIn — Variant 1 (Contrarian hook)

A manager once told me I should stop asking "why." Apparently it was considered aggressive. He had just done an NLP course. I thought about it. And I never really agreed.

Simon Sinek's Start with Why helped me understand what I was actually doing. Most organisations communicate outside-in: here is what we do, here is how, and if you're still listening, here's why. The leaders who inspire work the opposite way. Purpose first. Product second.

I apply this to every single project I walk into. Not just brand strategy. Every meeting, every kickoff, every consultancy assignment. Why are we doing this? What changes if we get this right? Before the architecture discussions, before the tool selection, before anyone draws a diagram.

In technical environments especially, the conversation accelerates fast. Very smart people reasoning at a sophisticated level. And then you're deep in solutions before anyone has checked whether the problem is the right problem.

The Golden Circle is not just for Apple and Martin Luther King. It is for every Tuesday afternoon when someone asks you to build something.

## LinkedIn — Variant 2 (Story format)

Early in my career, a manager pulled me aside. He had completed a course in Neuro-Linguistic Programming. His message: asking "why" is aggressive. I should rephrase. Approach differently.

I sat with that feedback. I never fully bought it. I just wanted to understand the big picture before the details. Why are we doing this? What problem are we actually solving?

Years later I found Simon Sinek's Start with Why. He calls it the Golden Circle: Why at the center, How in the next ring, What on the outside. The organisations that inspire — Apple, Southwest Airlines, King — all lead from the inside out.

What struck me was how directly this applies to every technical conversation I have. Teams come in with requests: we need a new platform, we want to modernise the stack. Push a little and a different picture emerges: we are losing engineers, we cannot respond to the market, the board wants visible value from technology.

Those are very different problems. They need very different conversations. And you only find them by asking why first.

## LinkedIn — Variant 3 (Question format)

When was the last time you paused a technical conversation to ask why you were having it?

Not the polite version. Not "let's align on goals" in the kickoff deck. The real question: why does this matter, and who actually cares about the outcome?

Simon Sinek's Start with Why is often read as a book about brand strategy. Apple, Southwest, Martin Luther King. Big visions, grand purposes. But the Golden Circle works at every scale. Every project. Every presentation. Every conversation where you are about to dive into solutions before the problem is truly clear.

In technical environments, the conversation accelerates fast. Smart people reasoning at a sophisticated level. Before long you are deep in how and what, and no one has stopped to verify the why.

That is usually where things quietly go wrong. Not bad technical decisions. Just the wrong problem being solved, very efficiently.
